Refugee Week Festival Kenya 2026: Courage in Unity – Shaping a Shared Future

Migration Pulse Hub and Umoja Refugee Creatives successfully co-convened Kenya's inaugural national Refugee Week celebration from 17–19 June 2026 at the University of Nairobi.

300–500

participants each day

102

households supported with SHA registration

16 + 17

refugee-led organisations and partners

The three-day festival brought together refugees, host communities, government representatives, refugee-led organisations, development partners, civil society, artists, students, and members of the public. It combined panel discussions, exhibitions, storytelling, poetry, cultural performances, networking, and community dialogue with practical access to services.

Participants received documentation and renewal guidance, Social Health Authority registration support, legal information, and referrals for education, livelihoods, and social services. Sixteen exhibitors presented art, crafts, digital technology, fashion, and culinary enterprise, while cultural performances represented communities from the Democratic Republic of Congo, Rwanda, Ethiopia, Eritrea, South Sudan, and Burundi.

What the festival heard

Across the programme, participants called for the Shirika Plan to move from policy commitment to practical implementation; for refugees to be recognised as leaders and contributors; for resources and decision-making to move closer to refugee and host communities; and for long-term investment in livelihoods, education, health, documentation, and refugee-led solutions.

Culture and storytelling were central to that work. Through film, poetry, sport, art, agriculture, entrepreneurship, and personal testimony, refugees shared experiences of displacement, resilience, identity, and belonging in their own voices—challenging stereotypes and building connections with host communities.

Commemorating 75 Years of Refugee Protection: Reflecting on the Past, Shaping the Future

Migration Pulse Hub, Amnesty International Kenya, and Umoja Refugee Creatives convened a hybrid webinar marking the 75th anniversary of the 1951 Refugee Convention.

Refugee leaders, legal experts, academics, humanitarian practitioners, civil society representatives, and advocates reflected on the Convention's legacy and the future of refugee protection in Africa. Speakers recognised the Convention as the foundation of international refugee law while stressing the need to localise international frameworks to African realities.

Africa's contribution and the unfinished work

The discussion highlighted the 1969 OAU Refugee Convention, the Kampala Convention, the Maputo Protocol, Kenya's Refugee Act 2021, and the Shirika Plan as important advances. Participants also identified persistent gaps: uneven implementation, encampment restrictions, barriers to dignified work and education, and limited meaningful refugee participation in policy decisions.

Looking ahead, participants called for stronger implementation of existing protections, greater refugee leadership, wider access to livelihoods and education, and more effective responses to climate-related displacement, digital protection, and other emerging realities.

The future of refugee protection lies not only in the existence of laws and policies, but in our collective commitment to translate them into dignity, opportunity, participation, and belonging for every refugee.

MPH and Umoja Refugee Creative Engage the National Coordination Mechanism on Migration Ahead of the Refugee Week Festival in Nairobi

Migration Pulse Hub (MPH) and Umoja Refugee Creative (URC) were pleased to jointly meet with the National Coordination Mechanism on Migration, led by Dr. Dan Opon, at their offices to discuss partnership opportunities ahead of the upcoming Refugee Week Festival in Nairobi.

This year's Refugee Week Festival will be the first event of its kind in East Africa, hosted in Kenya. It presents an important platform to celebrate refugee voices, promote inclusion through art and culture, and strengthen collaboration among actors working on displacement.

The meeting provided an opportunity for MPH and URC to introduce their work, share areas of collaboration, and explore how both organisations can contribute to a strong, inclusive, and rights-centred festival that reflects the dignity, creativity, and resilience of refugees, migrants, and host communities.

Together, MPH and URC are building partnerships that connect research, policy engagement, storytelling, creative expression, and community participation in advancing the rights of people on the move.
